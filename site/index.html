AI-related careers, late 2023 to Oct 2026

An exploratory, source-traceable look at how AI-related careers changed, comparing Thailand with the US, Europe and Singapore/APAC. It starts from no hypothesis and reports what each source says, including where sources disagree or where there is no data.

Demand: Indeed AI keyword share

This is the only series so far fetched from its primary source. It measures the share of Indeed job postings whose text contains AI keywords (for example "Machine Learning", "Data Science", "Artificial Intelligence"). It covers nine countries. Thailand, Singapore and other APAC markets are not in the dataset. A keyword match does not mean the job requires AI skills, and it counts titles that were relabelled as well as new demand.

Pay

Pay figures are self-reported (Levels.fyi, skewed towards big tech), recruiter-estimated, or modelled. Sources are shown side by side and never merged or converted. No FX rate has been retrieved yet, so there is no USD conversion. There is no reliable 2023 to 2026 pay trend in the data collected so far.

Verification log

  • Indeed US peak. A starting note said the US share peaked at 4.2% in December 2025. The current primary CSV shows December 2025 at 4.38% (monthly mean) and values continuing to rise to 2026-09. The CSV is used; the earlier note is not.
  • Indeed Q1 2026 Europe. A starting note (via a secondary blog) gave Germany 4.2%, France 3.3%, UK 2.7%, Netherlands 2.2%. The current CSV gives roughly 4.4%, 3.5%, 7.3% and 2.8% for the Q1 2026 mean. The UK figure differs most. The reason is not established (an earlier data vintage, a different metric, or a misquote); the secondary figures are not used.
  • Not reachable from the build environment: Stanford AI Index, Levels.fyi, Indeed Hiring Lab articles, Stanford Digital Economy Lab, BLS, Wayback, JobsDB. Figures from these are carried as confidence C until the primary page is read. Re-run fetching from an environment with open network access.
  • Indeed GenAI series. The tracker README lists GenAI_posting.csv, but that file returns 404 (retrieved 2026-10-12).

Method and limits

  • Every number is an observation with source, retrieval date, definition, confidence (A primary and method documented; B primary but self-reported or small sample; C secondary or unverified) and an estimate flag.
  • Metrics with different definitions are never plotted together. Missing years or regions are left as gaps.
  • Pay: self-reporting and survivorship bias, big-tech skew, ambiguous titles (the same title covers different jobs), recruiter figures are indicative.
  • Postings: duplicates and ghost jobs inflate counts; keyword matching is not skill requirement.
  • Entry-level evidence is US-centric. Thai data is thin: no Thai series in the Indeed tracker, and Thai posting counts depend on the exact search query and date.
  • Currency: no conversion until an FX rate with date and source is recorded.
