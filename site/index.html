AI-related careers, late 2023 to Oct 2026

An exploratory, source-traceable look at how AI-related careers changed, comparing Thailand with the US, Europe and Singapore/APAC. It starts from no hypothesis and reports what each source says, including where sources disagree or where there is no data.

Demand: Indeed AI keyword share

The share of Indeed job postings whose text contains AI keywords (for example "Machine Learning", "Data Science", "Artificial Intelligence"). It covers nine countries. Thailand, Singapore and other APAC markets are not in the dataset. A keyword match does not mean the job requires AI skills, and it counts relabelled titles as well as new demand.

Demand: share of postings with an AI skill (Lightcast, AI Index 2026)

A different metric from the one above: a posting counts when its text includes at least one of 300+ AI skills on Lightcast's list (10 skill clusters; the list is revised every edition, and an "agentic AI" cluster was added this year). Not plotted with the Indeed series. Values are comparable only within the 2026 edition: the 2025 edition reported different figures for 2024 (for example US 1.8% vs 1.65% here, Singapore 3.2% vs 2.83%). The set covers 22 economies; Thailand is not one of them. Source is the Our World in Data republication of the AI Index 2026 data; 2025 values for 11 economies match the report's Figure 4.4.2 labels.

Requirements and skills (US, Lightcast)

Counts of US AI-skill postings that list each skill, 2024 vs 2025 (a posting can list several skills). The AI Index notes that generative AI mentions grew 111% while their share of AI postings fell 5%, and that the share of AI postings mentioning ChatGPT, chatbot or conversational AI declined while agent-related and orchestration terms (for example LangGraph) rose. The "280%+" figure in the Lightcast blog is the agentic cluster's share of all US postings (0.06% to 0.23%); the larger percentages seen elsewhere describe single skills such as "Agentic AI" (151 to 16,541 postings). Only 2024 and 2025 are available; there is no 2023 skill baseline in the sources read so far.

Pay

Pay figures are self-reported (Levels.fyi, verified in tiers but skewed towards big tech), recruiter-estimated, or modelled. Sources are shown side by side and never merged. Local currency comes first; USD uses the ECB reference rate of 9 Oct 2026. Percentile ranges are shown where the source gives them, because one median hides a wide spread (for the US AI Engineer page, p25 to p75 spans $109,500 to $211,250). Rows with confidence C are hidden by default. There is no 2023 to 2026 pay trend in the data collected.

Entry level vs senior

The evidence is US only, from ADP payroll data, and descriptive rather than causal. The authors report no evidence of widespread economy-wide job displacement and no comparable gap for experienced workers. The earlier headline numbers (13% in July 2025, 16% in September 2025) came from a regression adjusting for firm-level shocks; the 15% and 19% below are the simpler descriptive shortfall the authors now emphasise.

Method and limits

  • Every number is an observation with source, retrieval date, definition, confidence (A primary and method documented; B primary but republished, self-reported or small sample; C secondary or unverified) and an estimate flag.
  • Metrics with different definitions are never plotted together. Missing years or regions are left as gaps.
  • Pay: self-reporting and survivorship bias, big-tech skew, ambiguous titles, recruiter figures are indicative.
  • Postings: duplicates and ghost jobs inflate counts; keyword or skill-list matching is not the same as a skill requirement.
  • Entry-level evidence is US-centric. Thai data is thin: no Thai series in the Indeed tracker or the Lightcast set, and Thai posting counts depend on the exact search query and date.
  • Currency: USD uses ECB reference rates dated 9 Oct 2026, applied to 2026 figures only.
